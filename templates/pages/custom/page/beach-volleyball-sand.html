{{!-- Wrong Sand Types Section --}}

Volleyball Sand is NOT Masonry Sand, Golf Course Bunker Sand or Play Sand

Example of Masonry Sand's Pea Gravel Content removed with VolleyballUSA.com's Grate Sand Device.

❌ Masonry Sand

AVOID
  • Typically full of dust, little pebbles, or both
  • 3-5% dust content on average
  • Some courts have 10% pea gravel content!
  • Results in dusty, cutting, packing sand

Do not use golf sand for volleyball courts!!!

❌ Golf Course Sand

TOO ABRASIVE
  • Made to pass the "fried egg test" with golf balls
  • Only half a golf ball should submerge
  • Opposite of what you want for volleyball
  • Results in scrapes, bruises, and injuries

Play sand contains excessive dust that creates poor playing conditions.

❌ Play Sand

TOO DUSTY
  • Designed for little kids to sit and play in
  • Lower price = corners cut on quality
  • Dust is not removed to keep costs low
  • Very noticeable dust when playing volleyball
